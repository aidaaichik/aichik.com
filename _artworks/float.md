There is a stillness in “Float” that feels like held breath. The manta rays move through deep water lit from within, as if something ancient is stirring just beyond what the eye can follow. They don’t swim so much as suspend — caught between weightlessness and intention, watched by other sea creatures drifting like witnesses. This is an invitation to a world with its own hidden order, a language the water speaks only to those patient enough to look.
